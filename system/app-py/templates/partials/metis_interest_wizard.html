Let Metis work it out with you

Writing your own interests into a box is harder than it sounds — people tend to put something too broad to be useful, or their job title. So instead, Metis will ask you questions: what you work on, what you'd read about even if it weren't your project, and what you'd rather never see again. Then she saves the answers herself.
You'll end up with two separate lists, because they do different jobs:

News interests — what you want to know is happening. Shapes the News tabs and your daily and weekly briefings.
Library interests — what you want a real scientific background on. Shapes which literature is collected and what the knowledge layer is built from.
They overlap, but they needn't match — and neither has to stay inside your work. You might follow a conflict in the news you'll never publish on, or want deep reading on a method that never makes the news. You also don't need any projects or papers set up first; if you're new here, Metis will just ask.
