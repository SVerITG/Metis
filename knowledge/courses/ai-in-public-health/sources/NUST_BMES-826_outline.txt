ABOUT THE COURSE
Applied Artificial Intelligence in Epidemiology (BMES-826) is a four-month, 3-credit certification course
offered for the MS programme in Biomedical Engineering & Sciences at SMME, NUST, and convened for
public-health managers, epidemiologists, disease-surveillance officers and laboratory managers.
Participants learn to build practical analytical tools for disease surveillance, outbreak prediction,
immunisation and vaccine logistics, and programme-performance monitoring.
The course integrates engineering, data science and health-systems perspectives, enabling learners to
work with heterogeneous, high-dimensional data — clinical, laboratory, surveillance, environmental and
immunisation (VPD), infectious-disease and health-logistics (cLMIS) datasets. It is built around three
flagship applied focus areas:
▪ AI for Surveillance & Outbreak Detection: building an Early Epidemic & Pandemic Detection System
   (EEPD) using anomaly-detection and time-series forecasting on multi-source surveillance signals for
   early warning and rapid response.
▪ AI for Health-System Readiness & Supply Chains: applying demand-forecasting and optimisation to
   immunisation logistics, commodity quantification and AI-FASP for outbreak and emergency
   preparedness.
▪ Comparative Pandemic Intelligence: using a Comparative Pandemic Intelligence Framework (CPIF) to
   benchmark multi-country preparedness and synthesise cross-national health-security intelligence.

  Applied, project-based delivery. Structured instruction is combined with hands-on computational
  labs in Python and R, scenario- and data-based quizzes, applied case studies built on real AI-in-
  epidemiology deployments, and guest sessions with practitioners.
  The course culminates in a capstone group project, where teams design and present a simulation-
  based decision-support tool addressing a real public-health challenge — outbreak early-warning,
  immunisation logistics or programme-performance monitoring.

COURSE CURRICULUM

MODULE 1 · FOUNDATIONS OF AI & THE HEALTH-DATA
ECOSYSTEM
Establish the epidemiology, health-security and data foundations for applied AI — from public-health impact and
high-risk pathogens to AI/ML principles, epidemiological methods and national and global health-data systems.


Week 1 · Introduction to Epidemiology & Public Health
Course orientation and faculty–student interaction, review of a previous capstone project, and the
scope and significance of epidemiology and disease surveillance for population-health action within
systems such as NIH and EPI.

Week 2 · High-Risk Pathogens, Biological & Climate Emergencies & Global
Health Security
The epidemiological profile of high-risk pathogens and biological threats, the intersection of climate
change with infectious-disease emergence, and global health-security frameworks for preparedness
and response.

Week 3 · Principles of AI, Data Science & Machine Learning
Core AI/ML technologies, terminology and assumptions, with an interdisciplinary engineering-and-
health-sciences perspective for framing analytical problems in public-health and biomedical
contexts.

Week 4 · Epidemiology Foundation & Data Ecosystem QUIZ
Measures of frequency, association and study design, and how to navigate structured and
unstructured surveillance, laboratory, immunisation and environmental data sources.

Week 5 · Global & National Health Data Systems QUIZ
Comparison of WHO DHIS2, HMIS, cLMIS and EPI/VPD registries; data-quality and interoperability
challenges; and their role in real-time surveillance and evidence-based decisions.

MODULE 2 · AI APPLICATIONS FOR DETECTION, MODELLING &
SURVEILLANCE
Move from foundations to applied modelling — building early-warning systems, applying statistical and machine-
learning methods, and running hands-on Python/R analysis on real and simulated public-health data.


Week 6 · AI Application — Early Epidemic & Pandemic Detection System
(EEPD) ASSIGNMENT
Design and evaluate an AI-enabled EEPD, applying anomaly-detection and time-series forecasting to
multi-source surveillance signals for early-warning and rapid-response decision support.

Week 7 · Statistical Learning & Classical Modelling in Epidemiology
Regression models, time-series analysis and compartmental models (SIR/SEIR) applied to
epidemiological data, compared with machine-learning alternatives on assumptions, performance
and interpretability.

Week 8 · Mid-Semester Examination
A written examination assessing epidemiological foundations, data systems, principles of AI/ML and
statistical learning covered in Weeks 1–7. (Not applicable to certificate participants.)

Week 9 · Practical Lab — Python/R for Epidemiological Data Analysis
Hands-on coding, model building and dataset exploration: end-to-end workflows covering data
cleaning, exploratory analysis, feature engineering and predictive modelling.

Week 10 · Machine Learning for Detection, Prediction & Surveillance
Supervised and unsupervised algorithms — classification, clustering and anomaly detection — for
disease detection, outbreak prediction and population-level surveillance, with epidemiological
performance metrics.

Week 11 · Advanced AI Tools & Applied Case Studies ASSIGNMENT
Deep learning, natural language processing and ensemble methods explored through real
epidemiological case studies, with critical appraisal of their advantages, limitations and ethics.

Weeks 15–17 · Capstone Group Presentations
Simulation-based presentation of a practical AI-enabled analytical or decision-support tool —
outbreak early-warning, immunisation logistics or programme-performance monitoring — with
structured faculty and peer feedback.

Week 18 · Final Semester Examination
A comprehensive written examination across the full breadth of the course. (Not applicable to
certificate participants, who complete quizzes, assignments and a final project technical report.)

APPLIED CASE STUDIES
The course is anchored in real-world, hands-on case studies so participants gain practical insight into
how AI improves preparedness, detection and response — while weighing the ethical and governance
considerations of deployment.
EARLY EPIDEMIC & PANDEMIC DETECTION SYSTEM (EEPD)
Build an early-warning system that fuses multi-source surveillance signals to flag anomalies and forecast
emerging epidemic and pandemic risk.
Skills you will learn: Anomaly detection, time-series forecasting, multi-source surveillance integration,
early-warning decision support


AI FOR DENGUE OUTBREAK PREDICTION
Predict dengue outbreaks by integrating climate, demographic and satellite data, with early-warning
systems and scalable public-health interventions.
Skills you will learn: Outbreak prediction (INLA, XGBoost, YOLOv4), explainable modelling, multi-source
data integration, governance


INTELLIGENT HEALTHCARE SUPPLY-CHAIN FORECASTING
Forecast demand and optimise immunisation logistics, commodity quantification and stockpiling for
outbreak and emergency preparedness.
Skills you will learn: Demand forecasting, optimisation, immunisation & cLMIS logistics, AI-FASP, health-
system readiness


COMPARATIVE PANDEMIC INTELLIGENCE FRAMEWORK (CPIF)
Benchmark multi-country preparedness and response to synthesise cross-national intelligence for
health-security decision-making.
Skills you will learn: Cross-national benchmarking, evidence synthesis, health-security intelligence,
decision support


GLOBAL & NATIONAL HEALTH DATA SYSTEMS
Work across DHIS2, HMIS, cLMIS and EPI/VPD registries, assessing data quality and interoperability for
real-time surveillance.
Skills you will learn: Health-data ecosystems, interoperability, data quality, real-time surveillance


EXPLAINABLE & EMERGING AI FOR PUBLIC HEALTH
Interpret and communicate model outputs to non-technical stakeholders and explore emerging
methods reshaping epidemiological analytics.
Skills you will learn: Explainable AI (XAI), stakeholder communication, GANs, large language models
